Dashboard

A live snapshot of your money. Everything here updates automatically whenever you add or delete an entry.

New here? How this app works
  • Step 1: Go to Add Entry and record each money transaction (or press Load sample data to see a demo).
  • Step 2: The app builds the Ledger, Trial Balance, Profit & Loss and Balance Sheet for you.
  • Not an accountant? Think of it as: money coming in (Sales, Income), money going out (Expenses), and what you own (Assets) versus owe (Liabilities).

Add a Journal Entry

Every transaction has two sides: one account is debited and another is credited, for the same amount. This is double-entry bookkeeping.

Help: how do I pick Debit and Credit?
  • Asset increases (cash, furniture, stock) → Debit it. Asset decreases → Credit it.
  • Expense incurred (rent, wages, purchases) → Debit the expense.
  • Income earned (sales, commission) → Credit the income.
  • Owner puts in money → Debit Cash, Credit Capital. Owner takes money → Debit Drawings, Credit Cash.
  • Not sure? Tap a quick template below and just change the amount.

Journal Book

The "diary" of your business. Every transaction is first written here in date order, with its debit and credit.

Help: reading the journal
  • "Cash A/c Dr / To Capital A/c" means Cash is debited and Capital is credited.
  • Made a mistake? Delete the entry and add it again. All reports will correct themselves.

Ledger Accounts

The journal sorted account by account. Each account shows all its debits, credits and a running balance.

Help: what is a ledger?
  • Dr balance = the account is on the debit side (assets, expenses). Cr = credit side (income, liabilities, capital).
  • Use the filter to look at just one account.

Trial Balance

A checkpoint listing every account's closing balance. Total debits must equal total credits.

Help: why does this matter?
  • If totals match, your entries are arithmetically consistent. If not, an entry has an error.
  • It is the starting point for preparing the final accounts.

Trading & Profit & Loss Account

Shows whether the business made a profit or a loss. Gross profit is from buying and selling; net profit is what remains after all other expenses.

Help: reading the result
  • Gross Profit = Sales − direct costs (purchases, wages, carriage inwards).
  • Net Profit = Gross Profit + other income − indirect expenses (rent, salaries, electricity, interest…).
  • Note: this version does not adjust for opening/closing stock. Enter stock adjustments through Purchases if needed.

Balance Sheet

A photograph of your finances: what you own (Assets) against what you owe plus the owner's investment (Liabilities & Capital).

Help: the golden rule
  • Assets = Capital + Liabilities. Both sides must total the same.
  • Capital shown here = opening capital − drawings + net profit.

Budget Planner

Set a monthly spending limit for each expense and see how much you have actually spent. Great for personal or small-business budgeting.

Help: how to use the budget planner
  • Pick an expense category, enter a monthly limit, and press Set budget.
  • Spending is read straight from your journal entries for the selected month.
  • Green = safe, orange = nearly used up (80%+), red = over budget.

Quick Guide & Glossary

Plain-language help for first-time users, students and anyone new to accounting.

Account types and where they go

Common words

Debit (Dr)
Left side of an account. Increases assets and expenses.
Credit (Cr)
Right side. Increases income, liabilities and capital.
Journal
The first record of every transaction, in date order.
Ledger
All transactions grouped under each account.
Trial balance
List of all balances proving debits = credits.
Gross profit
Sales minus the direct cost of goods sold.
Net profit
What is left after all expenses.
Drawings
Money the owner takes out for personal use.
